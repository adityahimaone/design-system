# Halation — Case 15

**Name** · Halation. The halo a bright edge leaves in the emulsion after the light has stopped.

**Category** · Interface identity for a 2026 product surface: reeded prismatic glass over warm
porcelain, ember at night.

**Audience** · Design leads and product engineers choosing a surface language for a screen that
carries photographs, colour and type at once — a media product, a creative tool, a commerce
front page. People who have already rejected the grey blur.

**Promise** · Every pane earns its blur: one refraction, one specular edge, one colour that
escapes. A surface that changes what is behind it instead of hiding it.

**Enemy** · The frosted-glass default of 2024–25 — a grey `backdrop-filter` panel with white type
on top, where the blur only dims the photograph. Glass used as a scrim, not a lens.

**Material** · Reeded (fluted) glass, 4 mm ribs at 9 mm pitch, over porcelain; the same ribs at
night over ember. The rib is the identity: it is why the field behind the pane arrives as
vertical bands of colour rather than a smear.

**The sentence the work can be rejected against** · If the glass does not change the colour
beneath it, it is a scrim, not a lens.

## Track

Constructed. The brand is a system — a pane, a rib, a spill — not an object. Fill only, no
strokes, no circles, one accent.

## Five physical colours

| # | Name | Value | Where it comes from |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Porcelain | `#fdf1ec` | the warm ground under the coral field |
| 2 | Ink | `#1c0b10` | the wine-black at the dark end of the ember asset |
| 3 | Coral | `#ff4a34` | the hottest rib in the prismatic field |
| 4 | Ember | `#f2892f` | the amber core of the ember asset |
| 5 | Obsidian | `#0b0409` | the blue-black the ember field falls into |

Lilac (`#cbb0d8`) is the third stop of the spectrum and is used as material, never as a job.

## Three defaults refused

1. **The grey scrim.** No `backdrop-filter` without a tint and a refraction. A pane that only
   blurs is banned by the enemy line above.
2. **White type on a photograph.** Small type never sits on the field; it sits on a solid plate
   beside it. This is the Field + plate recipe, made a rule.
3. **Glass everywhere.** Two panes per viewport, maximum. The third is a card, and cards are flat.
